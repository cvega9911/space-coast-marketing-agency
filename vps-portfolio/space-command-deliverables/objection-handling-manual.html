01 · The Philosophy

The Objection Handling Philosophy

Objections are not rejections. They are requests for more information.

When a prospect says "it's too expensive," they're really saying "I don't yet understand the value." Your job is to diagnose the real concern, not defend the price.

Rule #1: Never Get Defensive

The moment you defend your pricing, your experience, or your offer, you've lost the power position. Stay curious. Let the prospect discover their own pain points.

Rule #2: Validate Before You Answer

Always lead with acknowledgment: "That's a fair question." / "I hear that a lot." / "I understand where you're coming from." Validation disarms resistance before you address it.

Rule #3: Answer the Concern Behind the Words

Don't respond to the surface-level objection. Diagnose the real fear — is it risk? Is it ROI uncertainty? Is it switching cost? Respond to the deeper concern, not just the words spoken.

02 · Response Frameworks

The Three Response Frameworks

Memorize these three patterns. They handle 90% of objections without breaking a sweat.

01

Feel / Felt / Found

"I understand how you feel. Other [industry] business owners have felt the same way. What they found was [result]."

Works for: Price concerns, skepticism, "I already have someone."

Why it works: It validates their emotion, normalizes their concern through social proof, and pivots to a positive outcome — all without sounding rehearsed.

02

Question-Behind-the-Question

When they say [X], they're really asking [Y]. Respond to [Y], not [X].

Example: "It's expensive" → really asking "Will this actually pay for itself?"

Your move: Respond with ROI data, not price justification. Show them the math. Let the numbers do the convincing.

03

Third-Party Validation

"Don't take my word for it. Here's what [similar business] experienced."

Works for: Credibility concerns, results skepticism, "prove it" objections.

Requires: Case studies and testimonials ready to deploy. Always have 2–3 relevant stories in your back pocket.

💡

Pro Tip: Stack the Frameworks

You can combine them. Start with Feel/Felt/Found to validate, pivot to Question-Behind-the-Question to diagnose, then close with Third-Party Validation. Three layers of persuasion in under 90 seconds.

03 · Price Objections

Price Objections (The Most Common)

Price is rarely about the money. It's about perceived value. Here's how to handle the four most frequent price objections.

1
"That's expensive."
What they really mean: "I don't see the value yet" or "I don't have the budget"
Your Response
"That's fair. Most of our clients initially felt the same way. What they found was that the website paid for itself within the first 2–3 jobs it generated. Let me show you what I mean — here's what happened with [similar business]."
2
"I can get a website cheaper."
What they really mean: "Why are you worth more than Wix / Fiverr / my nephew?"
Your Response
"You absolutely can get a cheaper website. But here's what usually happens — it looks fine but doesn't generate calls, doesn't rank on Google, and doesn't show up on ChatGPT when people search for your service. What we build is a lead generation system, not just a website. Want me to show you the difference?"
3
"$1,500 a month is a lot."
What they really mean: "This is a significant expense and I need to justify it"
Your Response
"It is a significant investment. Let me ask you — what's one new customer worth to your business? [Let them answer.] Our CMO clients typically see [X] new customers per month. At [their number] per customer, the math works out pretty clearly. But it only works if we deliver — and that's why we have the 72-hour preview guarantee."
4
"Let me think about it."
What they really mean: "I'm not convinced" or "I'm avoiding saying no"
Your Response
"Of course. What specific questions do you still have that I can help answer?" [Wait for response. If vague:] "Is it the price, the timeline, or something else giving you pause? I'd rather address it now than have you wondering."
04 · Competition Objections

Competition Objections

Competition objections are rarely about the competitor. They're about risk of switching, loyalty anxiety, and fear of making a mistake.

5
"I already have a website guy."
What they really mean: "Switching feels risky" or "I have loyalty"
Your Response
"That's great — having someone who knows your business is valuable. What do they currently handle for you? [Listen.] A lot of our clients actually keep their existing relationships and use us for the things their current person doesn't cover — like AI visibility, Google Business Profile optimization, and ChatGPT rankings."
6
"I use Thryv / Scorpion / Wix / Square."
What they really mean: "I'm already paying for marketing"
Your Response
"How's that working for you? [Listen to their answer.] The reason I ask is that I actually used to sell Thryv here in Brevard. I know exactly what it does well and what it doesn't. The biggest gap I consistently saw was [specific gap based on their industry]. Is that something you've run into?"
7
"My nephew / cousin / friend does my marketing."
What they really mean: "I have a personal relationship I don't want to damage"
Your Response
"That's totally fair. Personal relationships matter. What I'd suggest — let me run a free visibility audit for you. If everything looks great, you've got confirmation your nephew is doing excellent work. If there are gaps, you've got data to have a productive conversation with him. Either way, you win."
05 · Trust & Credibility

Trust & Credibility Objections

Credibility objections are the hardest to overcome — because they're usually earned. Here's how to rebuild trust in real time.

8
"How do I know this AI stuff actually works?"
What they really mean: "This sounds like buzzwords"
Your Response
"Great question. Let me show you something." [Pull up ChatGPT on screen.] "Watch this — I'm going to ask ChatGPT 'who's the best [their industry] in Brevard County?'" [Show results.] "Notice who shows up and who doesn't. That's what your customers see too. Our audit shows you exactly where you're visible and where you're invisible across Google, ChatGPT, and local search."
9
"I've been burned by marketing agencies before."
What they really mean: "I don't trust agencies"
Your Response
"I hear that a lot, and honestly, I get it. The agency model is broken — long contracts, vague results, no accountability. That's exactly why we don't do contracts, we deliver a preview in 72 hours, and if you don't love it, you don't pay. You're never locked in. If we don't perform, you walk away. No penalties, no hard feelings."
10
"You're new — do you have experience?"
What they really mean: "Prove you can deliver"
Your Response
"Fair question. Yes, the agency is new. But I've spent the last [X] years advising Brevard businesses on exactly these problems — I was a business advisor at Thryv, sold ADP to local companies, and worked with dental practices at Hayes Handpiece. I know this market inside and out. And here's the thing about being new — you get my full attention. I'm not juggling 50 clients. You're one of my first, which means you get executive-level focus."
06 · Timing Objections

Timing Objections

"Not now" is almost never about the calendar. It's about urgency and priority. Your job is to create urgency without being pushy.

11
"Call me back in 3–6 months."
What they really mean: "This isn't a priority right now"
Your Response
"I'm happy to follow up then. But let me ask — what changes between now and then? [Listen.] The reason I ask is that every month you wait, your competitors who ARE visible keep getting the calls you're missing. Even if we just start with a free audit now so you have the data when you're ready, you'll know exactly what needs attention."
12
"I'm too busy right now."
What they really mean: "This feels like more work for me"
Your Response
"That's exactly why our model works for busy owners. You don't do anything — we handle everything. The only time commitment from you is this 15-minute call and reviewing the preview when it's ready. Everything else is on us."
07 · Dental-Specific · Socratic Addition

The Dental-Specific Objection Section

Dental practices have unique objections rooted in referral culture and front-desk delegation. These responses were built specifically for the Brevard dental market.

13
"I already get plenty of patients from referrals."
What they really mean: "Referrals are working, so why change?"
Your Response
"That's actually a great position to be in — it means you've built a strong reputation. Here's the thing about referrals though — they're capped. There are only so many people your existing patients know. Meanwhile, thousands of people in Brevard are searching for a dentist right now on Google and ChatGPT. If you're not showing up, they're going to your competitor. Referrals are great. Visibility makes referrals your floor, not your ceiling."
14
"My front desk handles marketing."
What they really mean: "I already have someone on this"
Your Response
"That's really common in dental practices. The challenge is that marketing has changed dramatically in the last 2 years — AI search, ChatGPT, local visibility algorithms. What worked even a year ago doesn't work the same way now. Our audit will show you exactly where you stand compared to competitors who ARE investing in this."
08 · The Golden Rule

The Golden Rule of Objection Handling

Never Argue With an Objection.

Validate, diagnose, redirect to value. The moment you get defensive about your pricing or your experience, you've lost. Stay curious. Ask questions. Let the prospect talk themselves into the sale by discovering their own pain points.

✅ Do This

  • Pause before responding. Silence is a tool.
  • Acknowledge with "That's fair" or "I understand."
  • Ask clarifying questions to diagnose the real concern.
  • Pivot to value — ROI, case studies, social proof.
  • Let them talk. The person asking questions controls the conversation.

❌ Never Do This

  • Get defensive about your price or experience.
  • Interrupt or talk over their objection.
  • Badmouth competitors or their nephew's work.
  • Discount on the spot — it signals your price was inflated.
  • Accept "let me think about it" without diagnosing the real hesitation.
🧠

The Psychology Behind the Golden Rule

Objections are emotional before they're logical. When you argue with an objection, you're arguing with their feelings — and nobody wins an argument about feelings. Validate the emotion first, then use logic to redirect to value. This is the difference between a salesperson and a trusted advisor.

09 · Pre-Call Preparation

The Pre-Call Preparation Checklist

Before every discovery call, run through this checklist. Preparation is the best objection handler — many objections never arise when you've done your homework.

  • ☐ Run visibility audit on prospect's website
  • ☐ Run visibility audit on their top competitor
  • ☐ Note 3 specific things their competitor is doing better
  • ☐ Have ChatGPT open and ready to demonstrate
  • ☐ Know their industry-specific pain points
  • ☐ Have pricing ready — state it as fact, don't hedge
  • ☐ Have Calendly open for scheduling follow-up
# Pre-Call Mental Model 1. Prospect says objection ↓ 2. You pause (1-2 seconds — use the silence) ↓ 3. Validate: "That's fair" / "I understand" ↓ 4. Diagnose: Ask a clarifying question ↓ 5. Respond: Use the right framework (Feel/Felt/Found, QBQ, or Third-Party) ↓ 6. Redirect to value: ROI data, case study, or live demo ↓ 7. Check: "Does that address your concern?"
✅

Remember: You're Not Selling a Website

You're selling visibility, leads, and revenue. Every objection response should circle back to the outcome — not the deliverable. Websites are the vehicle. New customers are the destination.
