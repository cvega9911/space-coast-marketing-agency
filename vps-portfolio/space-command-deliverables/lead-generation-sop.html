Section 01

Lead Generation Philosophy

The foundation that drives every sourcing decision. This isn't about volume — it's about precision targeting of businesses most likely to convert.

🎯 Quality Over Quantity

200 well-vetted leads beats 2,000 random businesses scraped from a directory. Every lead in our pipeline has been manually reviewed for fit, revenue potential, and digital maturity. We'd rather have 50 perfect-fit prospects than 500 tire-kickers.

📍 Brevard County Only

We are hyper-local by design. Target cities: Merritt Island, Melbourne, Cocoa Beach, Rockledge, Viera, Satellite Beach, Titusville, Palm Bay, Cape Canaveral, Indian Harbour Beach. No Orlando, no Daytona — we dominate our backyard first.

🔀 Two-Track Segmentation

Web Design Track ($599): Low-quality website, decent business, needs a modern online presence. CMO Track ($1,500): High-revenue business with bad (or no) digital presence — they need strategy, not just a website.

🧠 Lead Scoring Formula

Every lead is scored on three dimensions before entering the pipeline: Website Quality Score (1–10) — mobile responsiveness, design era, load speed, content quality. Revenue Estimate — inferred from location, employee count, review volume, vehicle fleet size, physical footprint. Digital Maturity — do they have a Google Business Profile? Social media? Are they running ads? This triage prevents wasting time on businesses that can't afford us or don't need us.

Section 02

Lead Source 1 — Google Maps Scraping

Google Maps is the richest source of local business data. Every business with a physical location is here, and many are severely under-optimized.

🔍 Search Categories

Run these searches with a location qualifier appended. Rotate through categories weekly to avoid duplication:

  • "plumber near me" + location: Melbourne FL
  • "dentist Melbourne FL" or "dentist near me"
  • "roofing contractor Brevard County"
  • "electrician Cocoa Beach"
  • "HVAC repair Titusville"
  • "landscaping company Viera FL"
  • "divorce attorney Brevard County"
  • "auto repair shop Merritt Island"

📋 What to Extract

For every business in the search results, capture these fields:

  • Business Name — exact name as listed
  • Address — full street address + ZIP
  • Phone Number — primary listed number
  • Website URL — if listed on GBP
  • Google Rating — star rating (1.0–5.0)
  • Review Count — total number of reviews
  • Category — primary Google category

🎯 Targeting Heuristics

Sweet Spot: Businesses with < 20 reviews. These are under-optimized — their competitors are outranking them, and they likely don't have a review generation system. Perfect for our GBP optimization upsell.

Filter Out: Chains and franchises (no corporate red tape — we want independent business owners who can say yes). National brands with 100+ locations. Businesses without a website at all (can't assess digital maturity).

💡

Serper API Method (for Socrates automation)

When the Mac Mini arrives, Socrates will use the Serper API with gl=us&location=Brevard+County+Florida to pull the top 20 results per category automatically. Until then: open Google Maps in a browser, run each search, and manually copy results into the tracking spreadsheet.

Section 03

Lead Source 2 — Yelp Business Directory

Yelp provides richer category filtering and review data than Google Maps. It's especially good for service businesses where reputation is the primary buying signal.

🔍 How to Search Yelp

  1. Go to yelp.com and enter a category (e.g., "Plumbing")
  2. Set location to a Brevard city: Melbourne, FL or Cocoa Beach, FL
  3. Sort by "Highest Rated" or "Most Reviewed"
  4. Scroll through results, skipping chains and franchises

Pro tip: Yelp's "Nearby" tab shows businesses clustered geographically — useful for identifying commercial pockets.

📋 Data to Capture

  • Business Name and Category
  • Yelp Rating (1.0–5.0 stars)
  • Review Count on Yelp
  • Website URL (if linked from Yelp)
  • Phone Number
  • Price Range ($, $$, $$$, $$$$)
  • Hours of Operation (signals active business)

🎯 The Yelp Sweet Spot: 3.5–4.0 Stars

Businesses with 3.5–4.0 stars are good but not great. They're competent enough to stay in business, but their online reputation is costing them customers. These are the highest-converting prospects — they already know reviews matter, they just need help managing them. Avoid businesses below 3.0 stars: that's a reputation crisis, not a marketing problem, and fixing it is a different (and much harder) engagement.

⚠️

Red Flag

Businesses with fewer than 5 total Yelp reviews are likely too small or too new to be viable prospects. They haven't proven market traction yet. Save these for a later phase when you're running Facebook ads to cold audiences.

Section 04

Lead Source 3 — Chamber of Commerce Directories

Chamber member directories are publicly available goldmines. Businesses that join their local chamber are established, community-minded, and have a marketing budget — they're already paying annual dues, so they're not allergic to spending money on business growth.

🏖️ Cocoa Beach Regional

Covers Cocoa Beach, Cape Canaveral, and the beachside communities. Strong in hospitality, retail, and tourism-adjacent services.

🔗 cocoabeachchamber.com

🏙️ Melbourne Regional

Largest chamber in Brevard. Covers Melbourne, West Melbourne, Palm Bay. Heavy on professional services, healthcare, and tech.

🔗 melbourneregionalchamber.com

🚀 Titusville Area

Space Coast-adjacent. Covers Titusville and north Brevard. Strong aerospace and industrial membership base.

🔗 titusville.org

🌴 Greater Palm Bay

Fastest-growing area in Brevard. Covers Palm Bay and southern Brevard. Mix of trades, retail, and growing professional sector.

🔗 greaterpalmbaychamber.com

✅

Why Chamber Leads Convert

Chamber member directories almost always include website URLs, phone numbers, and business descriptions. These are legitimate, established businesses — no shell companies, no fly-by-night operations. The chamber membership itself signals that the owner cares about their business reputation and community standing. When you reach out, you can open with: "I saw you're a member of the Melbourne Chamber — I work with several chamber members in the area..."

Section 05

Lead Source 4 — Facebook Group (Warm Leads)

Your 850-member local Facebook group is a warm lead engine hiding in plain sight. These people already know you, trust you, and engage with your content. They are the lowest-hanging fruit in the entire pipeline.

📢 The Free Audit Post

Post this once every 2–3 weeks in your group. Make it genuine — no pitch, pure value:

// Facebook Group Post Template "I've got some free time this weekend and I want to give back to the group. I'll do 3 free website audits for local business owners in here. I'll look at: • Mobile responsiveness & load speed • SEO basics (title tags, headers, meta) • Google Business Profile consistency • Quick wins you can implement yourself Drop your website link below or DM me. No pitch, no sales call — just a quick audit with actionable notes. First 3 to comment get it."

🔎 Member Profiling

Between audit posts, proactively scan your group:

  • Check member profiles for business pages linked
  • Look for members who post about their business in the group
  • Note members who engage regularly (likes, comments) — they're warm
  • Search the group for keywords: "looking for," "recommend," "anyone know a..."

Every person asking for a recommendation is a business owner's neighbor — and possibly a prospect themselves.

✉️ The DM Script — Group Owner Advantage

When you identify a business owner in your group, you have a natural opening that cold outreach can't replicate:

"Hey [Name], I run the [Group Name] group. I noticed you own [Business Name] — I've seen your posts in the group and I appreciate your contributions. I actually do websites and digital marketing for local businesses here in Brevard. Would you be open to a quick, no-pressure audit of your current site? I can send over a few notes — no pitch, just honest feedback."

This works because: (1) you're not a stranger — you're the group owner, (2) you opened with appreciation, (3) you offered value first. Conversion rate on these DMs is significantly higher than cold email or cold call.

Section 06

Lead Source 5 — Competitor Client Lists

The smartest lead source in the playbook: businesses that already pay for marketing. They're pre-qualified, budget-approved, and often underserved by their current provider.

🔎 Agency Portfolio Mining

Check these competitors' portfolio/client pages:

  • Thryv — massive SMB client base, many on templated sites
  • Scorpion — legal/medical focus, premium pricing
  • RevLocal — franchise-heavy but has independents
  • Local Brevard agencies — check their "Our Work" pages

Look for outdated designs in their portfolio — these clients are paying but not getting modern work. That's your opening.

🕵️ Client Logo Recon

Many agencies list client logos or testimonials on their site. For each client:

  1. Visit the client's actual website
  2. Check if it looks modern (or if it's been neglected)
  3. Run a quick PageSpeed test in Chrome DevTools
  4. Check their Google Business Profile for review recency

If the site looks like 2016 and the agency is charging premium rates, that business owner is a warm prospect for a better alternative.

✉️ The Poaching Approach

When reaching out to competitor clients, never badmouth their current provider. Instead:

"I came across your site through [Agency Name]'s portfolio — I do similar work locally. I noticed a few quick improvements that could boost your conversion rate. Happy to share them — no strings."

You're not selling against the competitor — you're offering a second opinion. The prospect draws their own conclusions.

🎯

Why This Works So Well

These businesses have already crossed the biggest hurdle: they've decided to pay for marketing. They have a budget, they understand the value, and they're not starting from zero. The only question is whether they're getting what they pay for. If their current provider is delivering templated, outdated work while charging $800+/month, your $1,500 CMO package with genuine strategy and modern execution is an easy upgrade conversation.

Section 07

Lead Scoring & Segmentation

Every lead gets a numeric score before it enters any outreach sequence. This isn't subjective — it's a weighted formula that removes guesswork and ensures consistent pipeline quality.

Scoring Rubric

Factor Weight Score 1–10 Notes
Website Quality 30% Mobile, speed, design year Score low = they need us more. A score of 2/10 on website quality with high revenue = CMO lock.
Revenue Potential 25% Infer from location, reviews, vehicles Physical footprint, fleet size, review volume, office location all signal revenue. A dentist in a medical plaza > a landscaper working from home.
Digital Maturity 20% GBP, social, ads? Do they have a claimed GBP? Are they posting on Facebook? Running any ads? More digital maturity = they already value online presence.
Category Fit 15% Dentist > landscaper for CMO High-ticket services (legal, medical, dental, roofing, HVAC) score higher. Low-margin businesses (lawn care, cleaning) can still work but lean Web Design Track.
Warmth 10% Connection, referral, group member Existing connection through chamber, Facebook group, or referral adds points. Warm leads convert 3–5x better than cold.

Score → Track Mapping

70–100 CMO Track · $1,500

High-revenue business, poor digital presence, strong category fit. These are your priority prospects. They need strategy, SEO, ads management, and ongoing optimization — not just a website. Pitch the full-stack CMO package.

Estimated close rate: ~15-20% with proper outreach

40–69 Web Design Track · $599

Decent business, bad website, lower revenue or narrow category. They need a modern site and basic GBP optimization. This is a volume play — ship sites efficiently, upsell to CMO later when they see results.

Estimated close rate: ~10-15% with proper outreach

< 40 Deprioritize

Low revenue, poor category fit, no digital presence at all, or unresponsive. These aren't dead leads — they're future pipeline. Revisit in 6 months or when you're running automated outreach at scale.

📋

Airtable Over Excel (When Ready)

Start in Excel or Google Sheets — it's fast, familiar, and free. Once you're past 50 leads, migrate to Airtable. The column structure above is designed to be Airtable-native from day one.

Section 09

Socratic Automation Note

This entire SOP is designed for manual execution today — but it's architected to be fully automated once the Mac Mini M4 Pro is online and Socrates is operational.

🤖 The Automated Pipeline

Once Socrates is running on the Mac Mini, the entire lead generation process becomes hands-off:

  1. Serper API pulls the top 20 results per category with Brevard County location parameters
  2. Gemma 3 (local) scores each website's quality from scraped HTML — evaluating mobile responsiveness, design era indicators, load performance signals
  3. Results auto-populate Airtable via the Airtable API — scored, segmented, and ready for review
  4. Chris reviews and cherry-picks the top 50 prospects for manual outreach

⏳ Manual Phase (Current)

Until the Mac arrives, everything runs manually:

  • Google Maps searches done in-browser
  • Yelp searches done manually per category
  • Chamber directories visited and scraped by hand
  • Website quality scored manually using the 1–10 rubric
  • Data entered into Google Sheets (migrate to Airtable later)

Estimated manual effort: 4–6 hours to build the initial 200-lead list. Worth every minute — this is the fuel for the entire agency engine.

🚀 The Automation Multiplier

The beauty of this approach: the manual work you do now directly trains the automation. Your scoring decisions, your category prioritization, your filtering heuristics — all of it becomes the training data and prompt engineering for Socrates. Every hour you spend manually prospecting today is an hour of automation you won't have to build tomorrow. The SOP is the blueprint; the manual reps are the calibration data.
