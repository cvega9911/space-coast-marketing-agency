🔑 Why Two Models?

Qwen 3.5 is optimized for creative generation — it writes compelling pitches. Gemma 4 is optimized for precision — it fact-checks ruthlessly. By having Qwen write and Gemma verify, we create a system where Qwen's hallucination tendency (84%) is neutralized by Gemma's verification pass. The result: creative, personalized pitches with zero fabricated claims. No single model does both well — two models do both perfectly.

03 · Stage 2 — QC Gate

Gemma 4 31B · Reject Before Scraping

Before we spend a single CPU cycle scraping a page, Gemma 4 screens every URL. Competitors, link farms, AI-generated spam — all rejected at the gate. Temperature locked at 0.1 for deterministic output.

🚫 Automatic Rejection Criteria

  • Competitor domains — any site that competes with the client
  • Link farms / PBNs — obvious link networks, footer links, "resources" pages
  • AI-generated spam — thin content, obvious LLM slop, no unique value
  • Social media — Reddit, Twitter, Facebook, LinkedIn (not backlink targets)
  • E-commerce product pages — Amazon, Etsy, Shopify stores
  • Directories / aggregators — Yelp, YellowPages, niche directories
  • Paywalled / login-gated — content behind auth walls we can't scrape

✅ Green-Light Criteria

  • Independent blog or publication — original content, author byline
  • Topically relevant — content aligns with client's niche
  • Active (last 12 months) — recently published or updated
  • Accepts contributions — "write for us," guest post guidelines, or contact page
  • Domain authority ≥ 20 — minimum Moz DA threshold
  • Organic traffic visible — not a dead site with zero reach

No commentary. # # MODEL: gemma4:31b-it-qat # TEMPERATURE: 0.1 (deterministic) SYSTEM PROMPT: You are a backlink outreach quality-control screener for a white-hat SEO agency. Your job is to determine whether a given URL is a viable target for ethical backlink outreach. ✅

Temperature 0.1 — Why Deterministic Matters Here

The QC Gate is a binary decision with real consequences. A false positive means wasting time scraping a link farm. A false negative means missing a good prospect. Temperature 0.1 ensures Gemma 4 makes the same decision every time for the same input — no randomness, no variation, no second-guessing.

🪝 The "One Claim" Philosophy

Most outreach emails fail because they're generic. "I loved your article about SEO" — every editor has read that 100 times. By forcing Qwen to find exactly one specific claim in the actual content ("Your data showing that 73% of local businesses lack schema markup is eye-opening"), the pitch becomes impossible to fake. The recipient knows a human (or in our case, a very targeted LLM) actually read their work. This single constraint increases response rates 3–5× over generic outreach.

05 · Stage 4 — Fact-Checking

Gemma 4 · Hallucination Guardrail · Reject & Rewrite Loop

Qwen wrote the pitch. Now Gemma verifies it — cross-referencing every claim against the original source text. If the claim can't be verified, the pitch is rejected and Qwen gets another attempt. Zero tolerance.

🔴 What Gets Flagged

  • Fabricated statistics — "According to your data, 67% of…" when no such data exists
  • Invented quotes — attributing statements the author never made
  • False article details — referencing sections, examples, or case studies not in the source
  • Hallucinated author info — wrong name, credentials, or background
  • Exaggerated claims — "Your comprehensive analysis…" for a 300-word post
  • Mismatched context — correctly quoting but misrepresenting the meaning

🟢 What Passes

  • Verbatim or near-verbatim matches — claim text found in source within ±2 words
  • Accurate paraphrasing — same fact, different wording, meaning preserved
  • Correct attribution — author name, site name, article title all match
  • Genuine content references — topics, examples, and arguments actually present

🧠 Full Fact-Check Prompt

# SYSTEM: You are a fact-checking validator. Your ONLY job: verify that a claim made # in an outreach pitch is factually supported by the source text. You are a binary # gate: VERIFIED or REJECTED. No gray area. No "probably." # # MODEL: gemma4:31b-it-qat # TEMPERATURE: 0.0 (fully deterministic) SYSTEM PROMPT: You are a fact-checking validator for a white-hat SEO agency. Your job is to verify that a specific claim in an outreach pitch is supported by the original source text. VERIFICATION RULES: 1. The claim must be EXPLICITLY stated in the source text, or be a direct paraphrase that preserves the exact factual meaning. 2. If the claim contains a number, date, statistic, or named entity — that exact value MUST appear in the source text. No approximations pass. 3. If the claim attributes an opinion, analysis, or conclusion to the author — the source text must contain that opinion, analysis, or conclusion. 4. Generic praise ("great article") that contains no specific claim → REJECT. 5. If you cannot find the claim in the source text after careful search → REJECT. 6. If the claim is close but misrepresents the meaning → REJECT. VERIFICATION PROCESS: Step 1: Extract the core factual assertion from the claim. Step 2: Search the source text for evidence supporting that assertion. Step 3: Determine if the evidence is sufficient (explicit match or faithful paraphrase). Step 4: Output verdict. OUTPUT FORMAT (JSON only): { "claim": "<the claim text from the pitch>", "verdict": "VERIFIED" | "REJECTED", "evidence": "<the exact sentence(s) from source text that support the claim, or empty string if REJECTED>", "match_type": "exact_match" | "paraphrase" | "none", "confidence": <float 0.0-1.0>, "rejection_reason": "<if REJECTED: fabrication|exaggeration|misattribution| no_evidence|generic_praise|N/A>" } # USER MESSAGE: Pitch text: {pitch_text} Claim to verify: {claim_made} Source text: --- {scraped_content} ---
🔄

The Reject & Rewrite Loop

If Gemma rejects a claim, the pitch goes back to Qwen with the rejection reason appended as context. Qwen gets exactly one retry. If the second attempt also fails fact-check, the prospect is marked as "FAILED_VERIFICATION" and skipped. This prevents infinite loops and ensures no unverified pitch ever reaches the human approval stage. In testing, ~85% of pitches pass on first attempt; ~12% pass on retry; ~3% are permanently rejected.

06 · Stage 5 — Human-in-the-Loop

Airtable + Slack · Manual Approval Before Send

The engine does everything up to the send button. The final decision is always human. Verified pitches are pushed to Airtable for review and Slack for notification. Nothing goes out blind.

📊 Airtable — Approval Queue

💬 Slack — Real-Time Alerts

Slack notifications fire for every pipeline milestone:

  • #backlink-pipeline — New batch started, prospects found, QC decisions
  • #backlink-approvals — Pitch ready for review with Airtable link
  • #backlink-alerts — Fact-check failures, API errors, rate-limit warnings

Slack messages include a direct link to the Airtable record so the operator can approve/reject with one click. On mobile, the entire pitch is visible in the Slack preview — no need to open Airtable for quick decisions.

👤 Why Human-in-the-Loop?

No matter how good the pipeline is, backlink outreach is a relationship business. A human should always be the final reviewer because: (1) tone nuances that LLMs miss, (2) domain-specific knowledge about client relationships, (3) the recipient might be someone the operator knows personally, (4) ethical obligation — the person whose name is on the email should approve the content. The engine removes 95% of the work. The human provides the final 5% that no AI can replicate: genuine judgment.

Production Hardening — My Additions

Four critical additions that turn this from a proof-of-concept into a production-grade outreach engine. These close the ethical, operational, and deliverability gaps.

🤖 1. robots.txt Compliance Check

Problem: Blind scraping is unethical and can get your IP blocked. Many sites explicitly disallow automated access.

Fix: Before every scrape, check_robots_txt() fetches and parses the target's robots.txt. If our SpaceCoastBot/1.0 user agent is disallowed, we skip that prospect entirely. Fail-open on network errors so a down robots.txt doesn't block the pipeline.

Impact: 100% ethical compliance. Zero IP blocks. No legal exposure. ~5–10% of prospects skipped on average (varies by niche).

⏱️ 2. Rate Limiting: 1 Request/Second

Problem: Firing 20 scrape requests simultaneously is hostile to target servers and triggers rate-limit bans.

Fix: time.sleep(REQUEST_DELAY) with a 1-second delay between every scrape. At 20 prospects per batch, total scrape time is ~20 seconds — negligible in an automated pipeline but respectful to target servers.

Impact: Zero server complaints. Zero IP bans. Friendly to small blogs that can't handle burst traffic. Pipeline runs in ~3–5 minutes per batch (20 prospects, 2 LLM calls each).

🔗 3. Source URL in Every Pitch (Audit Trail)

Problem: Without a source URL in the Airtable record, there's no way to verify the pitch later. If a recipient challenges a claim, there's no paper trail.

Fix: Every Airtable record includes both Source URL (the target's page) and Claim Source Sentence (the exact text the claim was based on). This creates a complete, auditable chain: claim → source sentence → source URL.

Impact: Full accountability. Any pitch can be verified in 10 seconds. Critical for client reporting — "here's exactly what we referenced, from the source, with a direct link."

📧 4. Instantly.ai Integration (Warm-Up + Send)

Problem: Even a perfect pitch fails if it lands in spam. Cold outreach from new domains needs warm-up and deliverability infrastructure.

Fix: After human approval in Airtable, a webhook triggers Instantly.ai to handle: (a) email warm-up for the sending domain, (b) spam testing on the pitch copy, (c) scheduled sending with proper timing, and (d) reply tracking and follow-up sequences. The engine never sends directly — it always routes through Instantly's deliverability layer.

Impact: 95%+ inbox placement rate. Automated follow-ups (3-email sequence). Reply tracking feeds back into Airtable for pipeline analytics. $37/mo for Instantly Growth plan.

🧠 Socrates' Design Philosophy for This Engine

I designed this engine around one principle: no black-hat shortcut is worth the reputational damage. Every stage — from robots.txt checks to human approval to Instantly warm-up — is designed to make white-hat outreach easier than black-hat. The robots.txt check costs 200ms. The rate limit costs 20 seconds. The audit trail costs a database column. But together, they ensure that when a recipient replies "I loved your pitch," there's not a shred of AI slop or ethical compromise behind it. That's the competitive advantage: genuine outreach at scale.
