01 · The Competitive Landscape

Where You Sit — And Why It Matters

Understanding the Brevard County marketing ecosystem is the first step to dominating it. Here's who's out there, what they're doing, and why exactly zero of them can do what you do.

The Brevard Marketing Ecosystem

Brevard County is dominated by Thryv — the national, publicly-traded business software company that sells an all-in-one SaaS platform. They have name recognition and deep pockets, but their product requires the business owner to do all the work. Then there are a handful of boutique agencies like Scorpion (national, enterprise-focused) and a few small local shops. Finally, the market is saturated with freelancers — solo operators charging low rates but delivering inconsistent quality with zero scalability.

Here's the reality: no one in Brevard County is doing AI-powered, locally-run marketing that combines the quality of an agency with the speed and personal touch of a local operator. No one is running frontier AI models on local hardware to generate content and audits. No one is talking about AEO (Answer Engine Optimization). No one offers a 72-hour preview guarantee. You occupy a unique, defensible position — and these battle cards are designed to help you communicate that in every sales conversation.

Tier 1 — Direct Competitors

Thryv, Scorpion

National players with big budgets. They go after the same local business market. Their weakness: impersonal, expensive, and slow.

Tier 2 — Indirect Competitors

Local Agencies, Freelancers

They compete on price and local relationships. Their weakness: no AI capabilities, no scalability, inconsistent quality.

Tier 3 — Silent Competitor

DIY / Wix / Squarespace

The "I can do it myself" mindset. Their weakness: massive time investment, amateur results, no strategic thinking behind the output.

🧠 The Strategic Insight

You're not competing in a crowded lane. You're creating a new lane: locally-embedded, AI-augmented marketing with agency quality at freelancer speed. The battle cards below are designed to help you articulate this difference without ever sounding like you're trashing the competition. When a prospect brings up a competitor, you don't attack — you diagnose.

Thryv

Thryv is your #1 competitor in Brevard County. They have brand recognition, a large sales force, and a publicly-traded company behind them. But their product is software — not service. And you know their weaknesses from the inside.

🏢

Thryv Weaknesses — Your Ammunition

  • ✕ Software requires the owner to DO the work. Thryv sells you tools. You sell outcomes. The business owner didn't sign up to become a part-time marketer.
  • ✕ Expensive, long-term contracts. Thryv locks clients into annual agreements with cancellation fees. You offer month-to-month with zero commitment.

When a prospect mentions they've used Thryv or are considering it, this knowledge becomes your single most powerful sales tool. You're not speculating — you're informing from direct experience.

If you've got time for that, it can work. But if you'd rather focus on running your business while someone else handles the marketing, that's where we come in. We do the work, not just provide the tools."

The Brevard market is full of solo operators — freelance designers, part-time marketers, one-person web shops. They compete on price and local relationships, but they can't scale and don't have the technology stack to compete at the level you operate.

👤

Freelancers — Low Price, Low Ceiling

LOW-MEDIUM THREAT
THEIR OFFERING

"I'll Build You a Website"

Low rates — often $500 or less. One person handling everything (when they're available). No strategic layer. No AI capabilities. Deliverable is usually just a website, not a marketing system.

YOUR OFFERING

"I'll Build You a Marketing Department"

Agency-quality output backed by an AI stack that scales infinitely. Strategy, execution, AI visibility, AEO audits — the full marketing function, not just a deliverable.

Freelancer Weaknesses

  • ✕ Can't scale. A freelancer can handle maybe 10–15 clients before quality drops. Your AI-augmented workflow scales efficiently without degrading output.
  • ✕ No AI capabilities. Freelancers use Canva, WordPress, and Google. They don't run frontier models locally. They can't do AEO audits. They're operating in 2019 while you're operating in 2026.
  • ✕ No AEO knowledge. Most freelancers don't know what Answer Engine Optimization is. They optimize for Google rankings — which is only half the battle.
  • ✕ Inconsistent quality. Without a system, output varies wildly. One project might be great; the next might be rushed. You deliver consistent, systematized quality every time.
💀 KILLER LINE

"A freelancer can build you a website. We build you a marketing department."

05 · Battle Card — Silent Competitor

DIY / Wix / Squarespace

The silent competitor — the prospect who convinces themselves "I can just do it myself." They're not wrong that Wix exists. They are wrong about what it actually costs them in time, quality, and opportunity.

🔧

DIY — The Most Dangerous Competitor (Because They're Invisible)

HIGH THREAT
THE DIY MINDSET

"I Can Figure It Out"

$20/month for Wix. Drag and drop. Templates available. "How hard can it be?" The answer: hard enough that most DIY sites look amateur, load slowly, and convert at 0.2% — if at all.

THE PROFESSIONAL REALITY

"You Run Your Business. We Run the Marketing."

Professional design. SEO-structured. AI-optimized. Fast-loading. Actually converts visitors into customers. And you don't lose 40 hours of your life wrestling with a drag-and-drop builder.

DIY Weaknesses — The Hidden Costs

  • ✕ Massive time investment. The average business owner spends 30–50 hours building a DIY website — and it still looks mediocre. That's time they could have spent generating revenue.
  • ✕ Amateur results. Templates look like templates. Customers can tell. A DIY site signals "small-time operation" — even if the business isn't.
  • ✕ No strategy behind it. A website without SEO, without conversion optimization, without AEO structure is just a digital business card. It doesn't bring in customers — it just exists.
  • ✕ No AI visibility. DIY platforms don't structure content for AI search engines. ChatGPT and Perplexity will never recommend a Wix site over a professionally optimized one.
  • ✕ Ongoing maintenance burden. Updates, security, backups, plugin conflicts — the DIY approach creates a never-ending to-do list that the business owner didn't sign up for.
💀 KILLER LINE

"You didn't become a dentist to build websites. Let us handle the marketing so you can handle the patients."

🧠 The DIY Objection Is Really About Value, Not Capability

When a prospect says "I can do it myself," they're not saying they're a better marketer than you. They're saying they don't yet understand the value of professional marketing. Your job isn't to argue — it's to reframe the conversation around what their time is actually worth. "How much revenue does one hour of your time generate? Now multiply that by the 40 hours you'd spend building a site. That's the real cost of DIY."

08 · How to Use Battle Cards in Sales

The Art of Competitive Conversation

Battle cards are weapons — and like any weapon, they're most effective when used with precision, not aggression. These rules govern how to deploy competitive intelligence in sales conversations without ever looking like you're attacking the competition.

1

Never Mention a Competitor Unless the Prospect Brings Them Up First

Volunteering competitive comparisons unprompted makes you look defensive and insecure. Wait for the prospect to say "We're using Thryv" or "We talked to Scorpion." Then you're responding, not attacking.

2

Use Diagnostic Framing, Not Direct Criticism

Never say "Thryv is terrible." Instead, say "A lot of our clients previously used Thryv. The feedback we consistently hear is that the software requires the business owner to do all the work. That's why we built a model where we do the work for you." You're not trashing the competitor — you're diagnosing a problem and presenting the solution.

3

The Script: "[Competitor] is a solid [product/agency]. The feedback we hear from clients who've used them is [weakness]. That's why we [your advantage]."

This three-part structure — acknowledge, diagnose, differentiate — keeps you positioned as an expert rather than a critic. You're not saying they're bad; you're saying they serve a different need, and here's what makes your approach different.

4

When They Mention Thryv: Your Insider Knowledge Is the Close

The moment a prospect says "Thryv," the conversation shifts. You now have something no other salesperson in Brevard has: firsthand experience selling that exact product. Lead with curiosity ("How's it working for you?"), listen to their frustrations, then deploy your insider knowledge naturally. "I actually sold Thryv for a while — here's what I learned about where it excels and where it doesn't."

5

Never Badmouth — Always Reframe

The fastest way to lose a deal is to trash a competitor the prospect chose or is considering. If they chose Thryv, criticizing Thryv is criticizing their decision-making. Instead, frame it as evolution: "Thryv was the right choice when the market looked different. But AI search has changed the game, and what worked two years ago isn't working now. Here's what's changed..." You're not saying they made a bad choice — you're saying the battlefield shifted.

6

Let the AEO Audit Do the Talking

The most devastating competitive weapon you have doesn't require you to say anything negative. When you show a prospect their AEO audit — and they see their competitors being recommended by AI while they're invisible — the data makes your case for you. You don't need to say Scorpion's SEO is outdated. The audit shows the prospect's site isn't being picked up by AI, while a professionally optimized site would be. The facts do the selling.

⚠️ The Golden Rule of Battle Cards

You win deals by being the better option — not by making the competition look worse. Prospects can smell insecurity from a mile away. When you're confident in your positioning, you don't need to tear anyone down. Let your advantages speak for themselves. The battle cards are here to give you the language and the logic — use them with the confidence of someone who knows they've already won.

🧠 The Meta-Principle

Behind every competitive question is an unasked question: "Why should I trust you instead of them?" The prospect isn't asking for a feature comparison — they're asking for a reason to believe. Your battle cards answer that question not by attacking competitors, but by making it obvious why your approach is fundamentally different, fundamentally better, and fundamentally lower-risk. Trust is built through competence, not combat.
